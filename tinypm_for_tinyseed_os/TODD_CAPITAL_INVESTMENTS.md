## What This Means for Negotiation

### Option A: Request Reimbursement

Ask Don to credit these expenses against future rent:
> "Don, I've invested about $3,500 in repairs and improvements to your property this year - the greenhouse plastic, cooler repairs, tree removal. Per the lease terms we discussed, capital repairs are your responsibility. Can we apply this as a credit toward next quarter's rent?"

### Option B: Use as Leverage for Rate Reduction

Instead of asking for cash back, negotiate a lower rate:
> "I've been absorbing capital repair costs that are typically the landlord's responsibility. In exchange, I'd like to discuss reducing the land flat rate from $6,000 to $4,000."

### Option C: Formalize Going Forward

Don't fight over past expenses, but clarify for the future:
> "Going forward, I'd like us to clearly define what counts as 'capital repair' vs 'maintenance' so we're both on the same page."

## The Legal Argument (From Don's Own Lease Draft)

**Section 8 - Capital Improvements/Maintenance:**

> "Capital costs for repair to buildings and equipment are borne by the lessor; maintenance costs by the lessee."

> "It is the responsibility of the lessee to maintain all buildings and equipment, repairing in timely manner as needed. This includes, but is not limited to cleaning and sanitizing coolers, lubrication, inspection, changing oil on motorized equipment, winterizing all plumbing including draining irrigation lines and pumps."

**The distinction is clear:**
- **Maintenance (Todd's)**: Cleaning, lubrication, oil changes, winterizing
- **Capital Repairs (Don's)**: Broken exhaust fan, failed thermostat, refrigerant, greenhouse plastic

**Your cooler repairs and greenhouse plastic are NOT "cleaning and sanitizing" - they are capital repairs.**
