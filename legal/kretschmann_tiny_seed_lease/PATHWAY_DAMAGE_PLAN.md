# Pathway Damage Control & Mitigation Plan
## Tiny Seed Farm — Todd Wilson
## Date: April 2026

---

## Overview

During the 2025 season, some field ends were tilled beyond the intended boundary, encroaching on pathways and driving routes. This plan addresses each affected area with specific corrective actions and preventive measures going forward.

## General Prevention Measures

- **All field boundaries will be marked with flags before any tillage operation begins.**
- Field end markers will be placed before each season and maintained throughout.

---

## Field-by-Field Plan

### I-SO (I field / SO boundary)

**What happened:** Tilled too far at the end of the I field, narrowing the driving path between I and SO.

**Corrective action:**
- Moldboard plow the displaced soil back up the hill to restore the pathway width
- Plant long-season crops in the restored area to hold the soil in place
- Seed a drive-resistant cover crop mix on the pathway to stabilize it for vehicle traffic

**While cover crop is establishing:**
- Close the route at the end of the I field where it meets SO — reroute traffic temporarily
- At the opposite end (I/J boundary), seed the same drive-resistant cover crop mix and reduce tractor traffic through that field space

## Drive-Resistant Cover Crop Mix (for I-SO and JS1)

**White Dutch Clover + Annual Ryegrass**

This mix is based on SARE's *Managing Cover Crops Profitably* (3rd Edition). Both species are recommended for the Northeast region, tolerate Zone 6, and germinate at 40F soil temperature.

### Why These Species

- **White Dutch Clover** (*Trifolium repens* — Wild White or Dutch White type): SARE identifies this as explicitly traffic-tolerant. The lowest-growing types (Wild White) best survive heavy traffic. Perennial — spreads via stolons, fills in over time, persists without reseeding. Fixes nitrogen. A farmer near Pittsburgh, PA successfully uses white clover living mulch on contour strip fields in the same climate zone.
- **Annual Ryegrass** (*Lolium multiflorum*): Fast-establishing nurse crop that holds soil immediately while the clover gets established. Extensive fibrous root system is well-suited for field strips and exposed areas. Quick germination in cool soil.

### Seeding Rates

| Species | Rate | Method |
|---------|------|--------|
| Annual ryegrass | 15–20 lb/acre | Broadcast or drill 1/4–1/2 inch deep |
| White Dutch clover | 4–6 lb/acre | Broadcast on surface or very shallow |

For small pathway areas, hand-broadcast and rake lightly.

### Timing

Seed in April 2026. Both species germinate at 40F minimum soil temperature.

- Frostseed the white clover early morning while frost is still on the ground (freeze-thaw works tiny seeds into soil naturally)
- Broadcast ryegrass the same day or any time soil is workable
- A rain event within a few days of seeding establishes good seed-to-soil contact

### Establishment Timeline

- **Weeks 1–3:** Ryegrass germinates first, greens up fast, holds soil
- **Weeks 3–6:** White clover germinates, begins spreading under ryegrass canopy
- **By midsummer:** Clover fills in as ryegrass thins or gets mowed
- **By fall:** Clover has a solid stand that persists through winter and thickens each year
- **Mow at 2–3 inches** once established to keep thick and traffic-tolerant

## Going Forward

This was a learning experience. The fields at Kretschmann Farm have irregular shapes and boundaries that are not always obvious from the tractor seat. Flagging before tillage is a simple fix that prevents the problem entirely. The cover crop seeding at I-SO and JS1 will both restore the affected areas and create a visible, durable boundary.
