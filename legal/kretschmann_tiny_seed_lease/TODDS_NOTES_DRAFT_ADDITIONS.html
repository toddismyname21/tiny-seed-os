CRITICAL

NEW NOTE — Article 10 (Remedy for Violation)

Place after Article 10.1 ("work in good faith to resolve any issues")
This is a significant change from the 2023 lease, which included a 14-day written cure period, specific notice requirements, and mediation before termination. I would like to restore that structure. Specifically:

• Written notice specifying the violation
• 30 days to cure non-monetary defaults
• 14 days to cure monetary defaults
• Mediation (Article 13) required before any termination
• Only material breach justifies termination

"Work in good faith" is a good principle but it is not a process. Without defined steps, either party could claim the other isn't meeting their responsibilities with no opportunity to fix it. The 2023 lease had this right.
CRITICAL

NEW NOTE — Article 11.5 (Perennial Crop Harvest)

Place after "Upon lease termination, Lessor has the right to harvest any perennial crops planted by same..."
I request that if the Lessor exercises this right, the Lessee be compensated for the establishment cost of those perennial crops — including plants, labor, soil preparation, and maintenance over the growing seasons prior to termination. Perennial crops represent multi-year investments that I make with the expectation of future harvests. If the lease ends and you harvest what I planted and maintained, compensation is fair.

Alternatively, I would accept removing this clause entirely and allowing me to harvest or transplant my own perennial plantings during a transition period.
CRITICAL

NEW NOTE — Article 11.6 (Improvement Compensation)

Place after "Other similar items of life expectancy >3 yr and value >$200 could be negotiated."
The 2023 lease provided two clear protections: removable improvements could be taken by the lessee, and permanent improvements were purchased by the lessor at value minus depreciation. I would like to restore both of those provisions.

I have caterpillar tunnels valued at $17,000-$31,000 that are portable by design. If the lease ends, I need the right to remove my own portable infrastructure. For permanent improvements — soil health work, irrigation modifications, any structural additions — I request compensation at documented cost minus reasonable depreciation.

The current language only covers greenhouse film. That does not reflect the scale of investment I am making in this property.
NEW

NEW NOTE — Article 12 (Assignment and Sublease)

Place after "Lessee shall not assign, sell or otherwise convey any of Lessee's rights under this Lease"
I understand your concern about maintaining control over who farms this land. I share that concern — I do not want someone here who does not share our values. However, an absolute prohibition on assignment means I cannot bring on a business partner, sublease unused greenhouse space during months I am not using it, or ever transfer the business I am building.

I propose: During the initial term, no assignment without your written consent. After year 3, assignment with consent not unreasonably withheld. Sublease of unused growing space permitted with written notice and your right to approve the sublessee.

This protects your interests while giving me basic business flexibility.

NEW NOTE — Article 3.1 (Permitted Use)

Place after "All use of the Leased Premises shall be consistent with the goals and purposes stated in the Recitals of this Lease."
I want to add clarity here. The Recitals express shared values, and I support them. But "consistent with the goals and purposes stated in the Recitals" is broad and subjective. I request adding: "Compliance with this Lease shall be measured by the specific, objective standards set forth in the Articles and Exhibits, not by subjective interpretation of the Recitals alone."

This does not weaken the Recitals. It ensures that any disagreement about whether I am meeting my obligations is resolved by looking at specific lease terms, not philosophical differences.
NEW

NEW NOTE — Article 3.3 (Agricultural Income Requirement)

Place after the 50% gross income requirement language
I am committed to farming this land as my primary enterprise. My concern is that a rigid 50% threshold could put me in technical violation after a bad weather year — hail, drought, flood — none of which are within my control. I propose one of the following:

(a) Change to: "Lessee's primary agricultural enterprise shall be conducted on the Leased Premises" — this captures your intent without a percentage that weather can violate.

(b) Keep the 50% but calculate it as a 2-year rolling average, with an exception for documented weather events or natural disasters.

I will seek professional counsel on this clause before signing.

NEW NOTE — Article 3.8 (Sundays)

Place after "work by lessee or employees on Sundays to be agreed upon in advance."
I have a Sunday farmers market commitment for 2026 which requires harvest and packing activity on the farm. I propose that routine agricultural operations — harvest, packing, irrigation, animal care, and other time-sensitive farm work — be permitted on Sundays without advance approval. Heavy equipment use, construction, and non-routine work would still require advance agreement. Emergencies are already excepted.

NEW NOTE — Article 13 (Dispute Resolution)

Place after the mediation clause
I agree that mediation through the PA Agricultural Mediation Program is the right first step. I would also like to add:

• If mediation does not resolve the dispute, binding arbitration before litigation
• The lease continues in full force during any dispute resolution process — no termination while we are mediating or arbitrating

This protects both of us from a situation where a disagreement escalates to termination before we have had a chance to resolve it properly.

The 2023 lease required a $500 refundable deposit. This clause now requires $1,000 plus 0.5% of the agreed value of all leased equipment. Based on the Exhibit B totals, that comes to approximately $1,850. NEW NOTE — Exhibit C (Requirements and Restrictions)

Place near the top of Exhibit C, or as a general comment
A few items in this exhibit appear to be adapted from a conservation easement template rather than written specifically for this lease. For example, Section III.A.5 references the "Wayne County Soil and Water Conservation District" — this is Beaver County. I flag this not as criticism but to make sure the language reflects our actual situation.

Specific concerns:

Section I uses "forever" — this should read "during the term of this Lease." A 1-year lease cannot impose permanent obligations.

Section III.B.3 prohibits storing "chemicals including but not limited to oil, fertilizers..." — read literally this would prohibit tractor oil, OMRI-listed fertilizers, and any organic pest control products on the premises. I request an exception for supplies reasonably necessary for agricultural operations and stored in compliance with all applicable laws.

Section III.B.7 gives the Lessor sole discretion to determine which plant species are "detrimental." Many commercial crop varieties are technically non-native. I request adding: "This restriction does not apply to agricultural crop varieties grown for commercial production."

The "Land Management Plan" is referenced throughout but never defined. I propose that this means the Annual Operating Plan (Article 5.1) combined with the OEFFA Organic Systems Plan.
