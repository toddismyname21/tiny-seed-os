## THINGS THAT WORK IN YOUR FAVOR

1. **Beginning Farmer Tax Credit (4.6)** — Lessor agrees to apply for PA Act 65 (10% of gross rent as PA income tax credit for first 3 years, up to $7,000/yr). Creates financial incentive for Lessor to keep you.

2. **Mediation first (13.1)** — PA Agricultural Mediation Program before litigation. Good and standard.

3. **Lessor handles capital repairs (7.2)** — Clear division: structural/major = Lessor, routine/minor = Lessee. Fair.

4. **Organic certification continuity** — Property has existing organic cert (3-year transition cost avoided).

5. **Greenhouse film reimbursement (11.6)** — If terminated before 4-year film life, Lessor reimburses proportionally. Good capital protection.

6. **Infrastructure access** — Packing shed, cooler, irrigation pump, equipment, 6 tractors — substantial infrastructure you don't have to buy.

## RED FLAGS AND CONCERNS

### 1. EXTREMELY SHORT TERM (1 year + 2 renewals)
Max 3 years. Either party can terminate by August 1. For a beginning farmer investing in soil building, cover cropping, perennial plantings, and customer relationships — **3 years is dangerously short.** The Recitals mention a "99-year lease" vision but explicitly state "Lessor has no obligation to select Lessee as the recipient." Aspirational language with zero legal weight.

**Recommendation:** Push for minimum 5-year initial term, ideally 7-10.

### 2. RENT IS HIGH — AND ACKNOWLEDGED AS SUCH
- Land: $500/acre/year (363% above PA average of $108/acre per USDA-NASS)
- Total: ~$32,380+/year before fuel and arrears
- Lease itself admits this is premium pricing (4.4)
- The 50% gross income requirement (Exhibit C, II.1) means you must generate at least ~$65,000 from the site just to be compliant

### 3. NO TERMINATION PROTECTION FOR LESSEE IMPROVEMENTS
- Section 11.5: Lessor can harvest YOUR perennial crops for one year after termination
- No compensation for soil improvements, cover crop investments, or established customer goodwill
- You're required to maintain 90% cover crop (6.1), build soil, enhance carbon — all increasing LESSOR's land value with no equity stake

**Recommendation:** Add a "tenant improvement compensation" clause: if terminated by Lessor without cause, Lessee compensated for (a) value of perennial crops, (b) documented soil health improvements, (c) reasonable transition costs.

### 4. AESTHETIC CONTROL IS EXTREMELY BROAD
- Section 5.5: Improvements must use "living and natural materials" — grass paths over gravel, wood over metal, slate over other roofing
- Section 6.4: Refuse storage, vehicle limits, outdoor machine limits (4 machines, 500 sq ft, 6' height)
- Section 6.5: Tractors "routinely parked in buildings"
- Section 3.8: Sunday work requires advance agreement (Lessor lives on-site)

"Aesthetically pleasing" is not defined and is inherently subjective. A disagreement over parking could become a lease violation.

**Recommendation:** Define aesthetic standards with specific, measurable criteria. Add "reasonable" qualifiers with Lessee's agricultural needs as primary consideration.

### 5. THE ARREARS SITUATION (Article 9)
- Prior balance from 2025 — amount not finalized ("preliminary")
- Overcharged in 2025 (12 acres vs 6 actual) — only $1,000 credit for what should be ~$3,000
- $2,000/month toward arrears PLUS current rent (~$2,215/mo + housing)
- Total monthly obligation: potentially **$4,215+** before selling a single vegetable
- Section 9.3: Full payoff within 1 week of FSA loan proceeds

**Recommendation:** Get arrears finalized IN WRITING before signing. Don't sign with an undefined debt obligation.

### 6. ASSIGNMENT PROHIBITION IS ABSOLUTE (12.1)
Cannot assign, sell, or sublease ANY rights:
- Can't bring on a business partner
- Can't sublease unused greenhouse space
- Can't transfer lease if selling the farm business
- No succession planning possible

**Recommendation:** Add exception for assignment with Lessor's written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld).

### 7. NO CLEAR DEFAULT/TERMINATION PROCESS
Article 10 is just "work in good faith." No:
- Definition of default
- Cure period (time to fix a violation)
- Written notice requirement
- Distinction between material and minor breaches
- Protection against arbitrary termination

**Recommendation:** Add proper default clause: written notice, 30-day cure for non-monetary, 10-day for monetary, termination only for material uncured breaches.

### 8. INSURANCE REQUIREMENTS
- $1M liability naming Lessor as Additional Insured (standard)
- Personal property escrow $1,000 + 0.5% of equipment value OR insurance
- Historic barn indemnification — barns are essentially uninsurable, Lessor can "prohibit hazardous or incompatible uses" without definition

## MISSING FROM THE LEASE

1. **Right of first refusal** — No right to match an offer if property is sold
2. **Lease recording** — No provision to record, which would protect you if property transfers
3. **Force majeure** — No clause for natural disasters, pandemic, etc.
4. **Crop insurance provisions** — Who is responsible? What happens to rent if crops fail?
5. **Water rights specifics** — "Finite water capacity" mentioned but no allocation quantified
6. **Access guarantees** — Lessor lives on-site, controls shared spaces. No guaranteed access hours
7. **Rent adjustment mechanism** — No inflation index, no cap on increases at renewal

## NEGOTIATING PRIORITIES

| Priority | Item | Why |
|----------|------|-----|
| 1 | Longer initial term (5-7 years) | Can't build a farm business on 1-year renewals |
| 2 | Default/cure period clause | Protection against arbitrary termination |
| 3 | Tenant improvement compensation | You're building equity in someone else's land |
| 4 | Finalize arrears amount | Don't sign with an undefined debt |
| 5 | Assignment with consent | Business flexibility |
| 6 | Defined aesthetic standards | Remove subjective enforcement triggers |
| 7 | Right of first refusal | Long-term security |

---

## BOTTOM LINE

The Lessor clearly cares about the land — the Recitals are unusually thoughtful. The infrastructure package is genuinely valuable. But the legal protections are heavily tilted toward the Lessor: short term, broad aesthetic control, no default process, no improvement compensation, absolute assignment prohibition, and premium pricing. You'd be investing significantly in soil, systems, and customer relationships on land you could lose with 7 months' notice.

The bones of a good arrangement are here, but it needs rebalancing to protect your investment as the farmer doing the work.
