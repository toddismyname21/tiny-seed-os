Subject: We Need to Talk About How We Work Together


Don,

I need to have an honest conversation with you about the way things are going. I want to be upfront and transparent, just like I was when I wrote to you about this same issue last March.

I want to start by saying what I have always said. I am grateful to be here. I know what it means to be trusted with land you have put your life into and I do not take that lightly. You have been generous with your time, your tools, and your knowledge, and I have learned a great deal from someone who knows this farm as intimately as you do. That has not changed.

But the situation has.

Last March I wrote to you about needing more autonomy. I was respectful and I meant every word. I asked for the freedom to manage my own fields, make my own planting decisions, and follow my own schedule. That was over a year ago. The same problems I raised then have not improved. They have gotten significantly worse, and I now have a full year of financial data that shows the damage.

The Numbers

My spring sales have collapsed. From March through May I went from $54,310 in 2022 to $22,449 in 2023, to $19,886 in 2024, to $2,362 in 2025. That is a 96% decline. In spring 2025 I had virtually zero vegetable revenue. The only spring income I had was from flowers. That decline happened in the face of rising costs, scaling expenses, and the very real cost of the migrant labor I brought on to grow this operation.

My salad mix is my most important crop. It went from $28,592 in 2022 to $10,839 in 2025 because I was unable to get into the market in early May when I usually establish my presence for the season.

My late fall and winter sales dropped from $17,182 in 2022 to $7,269 in 2025, a 58% decline. Late fall crops depend on timely summer planting and space in my tunnels.

I rely heavily on my spring sales to pay for my spring expenses and the fall sales are generally money that I am able to pay myself to live. When I cannot get into the field on time, the whole season suffers.

The Pattern

I have been building Tiny Seed Farm for almost ten years. Every step has been incremental, hard-earned progress. Coming to Kretschmann Farm is supposed to be the next advancement of my business and the "security of tenure" you so eloquently noted in the recitals of your 2026 lease proposal. I came here for the land, the infrastructure, your collaboration, and the opportunity to grow.

The last two years you have restricted when I can get into my fields. This has been particularly harmful because I do not have anything else growing at another site. You want the cover crops to be bigger before I till. I understand that perspective and I respect the soil health practices.  Now that I see the real world results of my field management, I can improve moving forward, and I think that might require a wider discussion, but the result is that my planting windows close, my successions stack up, and I lose the early market opportunities that my business depends on.

Last year my instincts were to go into the fields and you vetoed my entry. Then it rained and rained and rained and I had no vegetable sales until June. My work stacked on top of itself. Successions that should have been weeks apart came on at the same time. My planning is not the problem. It is my inability to follow a plan.

I want to be able to plow a third of a field or half of a field and allow the other half to continue growing in cover crops. My scale is not the same as yours was and your methods sometimes actively make managing the fields more difficult for me.

Your own planting records show that you were planting spinach, carrots, beets, and peas by the first week of April on average. In four of those ten years you were in the field in March. It is now April 2026 and I am still waiting for the go-ahead on my fields.

Suggestions That Feel Like Demands

On top of the field access restrictions, I am dealing with suggestions that feel like demands. You were frustrated that I had not purchased a fan for a heater that was not needed at the moment and was not in my budget. You want me to buy plastic for three greenhouses all at once, well over $2,000, when I am trying to recover financially. You make suggestions about what I should plant and when I should plant it without knowing my market or my cashflow. When I do something on my own schedule, the response is "why now? You could have done this any time in the last six months." I am doing it when I am doing it because that is when it is a priority for me.

My Greenhouses

I worked hard to obtain access to the greenhouse infrastructure. Greenhouses are high investment, high value tools. You know that very well. But you are limiting and restricting me from using my own hard-earned assets to enhance my business and build in the resiliency I need to survive in a very challenging market. I would like you to think about where I may put my 30x100 high tunnel, and modular catapillar tunnels. This year I have no spring flower sales because you vetoed our opportunity to get overwintered flowers in the ground last fall. That is lost revenue I cannot get back. The labor, the seed, the lost sales are all gone.

The Greenhouse

You questioned why the greenhouse plastic took so long. I was nervous to change the plastic before the trees came down. I took down the trees when I did because that is when I had the time in my schedule. It took me as long as it did after that because I had to lay off Juan Pablo and his son to cut labor because of my expenses and cashflow. That is the reality of my financial situation and the flow of my seasonal labor.

What I Am Asking For

I said it last March and I am saying it again now. You and I run completely different businesses. You are trying to make me run mine the way you ran yours. I am not Kretschmann Farm. I have different markets, different customers, different products, and different cashflow needs. My farmers markets start in April and I need product at my early markets. I need that cashflow to sustain my business.

I am grateful for what you have given me and I want this to work. But I am asking for a collaborative relationship instead of a combative one. Your decisions about my operation are costing my business real money and creating harder conditions than necessary. I am not asking to do anything you did not do yourself on this same land for decades. I am asking for the same freedom to farm that you had.

I care deeply about what we are doing here and I want to keep growing it in a way that works long term. I would like to sit down and talk about this in person.

Todd
