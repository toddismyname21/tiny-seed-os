Subject: Field Management — I Need to Run My Own Planting Schedule


Don,

I need to talk to you about my planting schedule and field management.

My spring vegetable sales have dropped significantly since I started farming here. This spring I have had almost no vegetable revenue — my spring income in 2025 was almost entirely flowers. I went back through my QuickBooks records and the numbers are clear: my spring sales have fallen from over $54,000 in 2022 to under $2,400 in 2025. That is a 96% decline. And here we are in 2026 — I am still not in the fields, and this spring I have no flower sales either because we were not able to plant them last fall.

A major factor is timing. When I cannot get into my fields to till and plant on schedule, my successions get pushed back, they overlap, and I lose market windows. Right now I am behind on crops that should already be in the ground. The delays are stacking work on top of work and compressing my entire season.

I looked at your planting history spreadsheet — the Master Schedule you kept from 2004 through 2013. In those 10 years, you were planting spinach, carrots, beets, and parsley by the first week of April on average. In four of those years you were in the field in March — peas on March 18 in 2009, spinach on March 21 in 2011, beets and carrots on March 27 in 2012. It is now April 2026 and I am still waiting for permission to get into my fields.

I am asking you to let me manage my own fields and my own planting schedule. I know this land. I respect the soil health practices and I follow them. But I need to make my own decisions about when to till, when to plant, and how to manage my crop plan. The current arrangement is not working for my business.

I am not asking to do anything you did not do yourself on this same property for decades. I am asking for the same freedom to farm that you had.

I would like to sit down and talk about this in person.

Todd
