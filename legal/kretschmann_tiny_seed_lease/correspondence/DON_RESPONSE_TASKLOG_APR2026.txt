Just a few comments to pathway remediations, 1. Wouldn't want any tilling of the path—more harm than good in that it could easily destroy any remaining rhizomes for existing grasses.  Maybe just plan to scratch the surface lightly with a rake, broadcast seed, rake in, mulch with fine straw or grass clips 2. Might think of more mix of seeds for this.  Fescue, Bermuda grass, and maybe bluegrass along with sweet clover.  Also use perennial ryegrass or mix of annual and perennial. 3. Return traffic only when well established—6 wk or when 3" and roots established.  Very little if any traffic first year.  I think I have some old grass seed mixes (might not have original germ, but couldn't hurt)  Old farm wisdom was to use the sweepings from a haymow in the barn to seed such. Lots of grass seeds in hay!

Handbook is a great start, thanks for the shout out to the Kretschmann farm.  I'd add that it's also a beautiful place in addition to being productive.

1. Parking not addressed at all. Limit cars on site. Also encourage car pooling.  See #14 in attached

2. Sundays?

3. Good you list returning tools after jobs; perhaps extend that to cleaning up everything after jobs  so there's an incentive to finish the job. One rarely can resume a job as you expected to when left unfinished.

4. Would be good to specifically encourage attention to the beauty side of farming.  Enjoy where you are—others with indoor jobs envy you. When taking walks or riding to and from, pay attention to beauty and appreciate (in addition to crop observation).  Also habitually picking up little bits of trash—like plastic, water bottles, drip tape, etc.

5. Well water spigot only to fill a waterbottle, not big containers etc.

6. Would like not to lead people to think farm is somewhere to learn to drive a clutch or quickly learn how to operate a tractor. Though clutched vehicles are becoming rarer every day, encourage employees to learn to drive a clutched vehicle before operating a tractor.  There's enough new to be cognizant of without that one. Also, I'd like to ensure that it's either you or I, or someone with a pretty good experience who's doing the instructing on tractors, not simply someone who's the supervisor and operated one for a month or two. We've got so many dangerous situations on tractors which take quick thinking and experienced reactions to deal with. Better to have a few good people rather than lots with a little experience.

5. Education.  Add reading free subscription to American Vegetable Grower, Growing for Market…; sign up with Penn State Extension—they send all kinds of information; look at Cornell website.

6. I didn't see much specific food safety training.  I have a bunch of materials for that including a DVD in Spanish and English. Also all the logs required.
