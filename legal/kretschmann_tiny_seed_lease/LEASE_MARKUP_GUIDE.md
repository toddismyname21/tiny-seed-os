#### THE HIDDEN FORMULA (discovered in Don's own spreadsheet)

**Don's pricing formula is NOT straight depreciation.** It is:

> Annual cost = (Value ÷ Life) + **(5% × Value)**

The 5% is an undisclosed reserve/markup ON TOP of depreciation. It's not mentioned in the lease. Todd sees monthly dollar amounts in Exhibit B but has no idea that a significant portion is this 5% surcharge.

**Impact examples:**

| Item | Value | Depreciation/yr | 5% Reserve/yr | Don Charges/yr | Markup % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barn ($50K, 30yr, 59% use) | $50,000 | $983/yr | $1,475/yr | $2,459/yr | 150% over depreciation |
| Allis G ($3.5K, 44yr, 100%) | $3,500 | $80/yr | $175/yr | $255/yr | 220% over depreciation |
| MF 296 ($20K, 16yr, 89%) | $20,000 | $1,113/yr | $890/yr | $2,003/yr | 80% over depreciation |

**The 5% markup adds ~$4,240/year to buildings and tractors alone.** For longer-lived items (barn, old tractors), the 5% reserve EXCEEDS the depreciation — Todd pays more in the hidden surcharge than in actual value recovery.

**This is not inherently unfair — but it's not disclosed.** Article 4.5 says "valuations are much below replacement costs" but never mentions the 5% on top of depreciation. Todd should know this formula exists.

## DON'S THREE JUSTIFICATIONS FOR $500/ACRE — AND WHY THEY DON'T HOLD UP

### Justification 1: "If you owned the land you'd pay taxes on it"

**What Don charges:** $500/acre/year

**What Don actually pays in property taxes:** ~$15-25/acre/year (under PA Act 319 Clean and Green)

Don's farm is almost certainly enrolled in Act 319 — Pennsylvania's preferential agricultural assessment. An 80-acre certified organic farm qualifies automatically. Under Act 319, farmland is assessed at agricultural USE value (~$50-100/acre), not fair market value (~$8,500-9,500/acre). Beaver County total millage is ~18.67 mills. On use-value assessment, that works out to roughly $15-25/acre in taxes.

Even WITHOUT Act 319 (full fair market value), the tax would be ~$150-200/acre. Still less than half what Don charges.

And Todd's lease PROTECTS Don's Act 319 enrollment. If Don stops farming or leasing to a farmer, he faces 7 years of rollback taxes plus 6% interest. Todd being there keeps Don's taxes low.

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| Don charges | $500/acre |
| Actual property tax (Act 319) | $15-25/acre |
| Actual property tax (NO Act 319) | $150-200/acre |
| PA average farm rent | $108/acre |

**How to say it:** "Don, I respect that land ownership has costs. But under Clean and Green, farmland taxes in Beaver County run $15-25 per acre. My being here actually protects your Act 319 enrollment. If anything, I'm helping keep your taxes low."

### Justification 2: "The acres are irrigated — that's unusual and justifies premium pricing"

Todd already pays for irrigation:
- **Equipment & Machinery rent: $5,684/year** — includes irrigation pump and equipment
- **Electricity: $1,800/year** — runs the pump
- Water comes from the property — no water bill

Don is charging Todd for the irrigation infrastructure through equipment rent AND using irrigation to justify premium land pricing. That's charging twice for the same thing.

Even ignoring the double-charge: USDA data shows irrigated farmland rent premium in eastern states runs 20-40% above dryland. A 40% premium on PA's $108/acre average = **$151/acre.** Not $500.

And western PA gets 38-42 inches of rainfall annually. Irrigation is supplemental here, not make-or-break like out west. It adds value — but not 5x value.

| Claim | Reality |
|---|---|
| Don charges (justified by irrigation) | $500/acre |
| PA average + 40% irrigation premium | $151/acre |
| Todd already pays for irrigation via | Equipment rent ($5,684) + electricity ($1,800) |

**How to say it:** "Don, I agree irrigation adds value. But I'm already paying for the pump through my equipment rent and the electricity to run it. I can't pay for irrigation twice — once in equipment rent and again in land rent."

### Justification 3: "I don't want to give you an unfair advantage in the market"

1. **Todd pays $500/acre. PA average is $108/acre.** Todd has a $392/acre DISADVANTAGE. A reduced rate doesn't create an unfair advantage — it reduces an unfair disadvantage.

5. **Don and his family farmed this land for decades** with established organic certification, customer relationships, and infrastructure that took generations to build. Todd started from zero. A fair land rate doesn't give Todd an unfair advantage — it gives him a fighting chance.

**How to say it:** "Don, I'm paying $500 an acre when the state average is $108. I don't have an unfair advantage — I have a significant disadvantage. The state created the Beginning Farmer Tax Credit because beginning farmers need help competing. That's not unfair. That's the point."
