## Key Findings

1. **Spring revenue collapsed 96%** from $54,310 (2022) to $2,362 (2025). This is the single largest factor in the overall revenue decline.

2. **Spring 2025 had zero vegetable sales** — only flowers. This is consistent with restricted field access during the critical spring planting window.

3. **Late fall/winter dropped 58%**, from $17,182 to $7,269. Late-planted fall crops (which depend on timely summer planting) are also affected.

4. **Summer is the only season that grew** — from $14,122 to $33,417. This may reflect a shift toward summer-harvested crops that don't require early spring field access.

5. **Total revenue down 57%** over 4 years ($128,133 → $54,801).

6. **Don's own planting records (2004–2013)** show he was regularly in the field by early April and sometimes in March. See DON_PLANTING_HISTORY_ANALYSIS.md.

---

## Context

Todd Wilson (Tiny Seed Farm LLC) began farming on Don Kretschmann's property in 2023. Don has been restricting Todd's field access in spring, including forbidding tilling until cover crops reach a certain size. Don's own 10-year planting history shows he regularly planted spinach, carrots, beets, peas, onions, and parsley in the first week of April — and in 4 out of 10 years, in March.

The sales data shows a direct correlation between the spring planting restrictions and the collapse of spring vegetable revenue.
