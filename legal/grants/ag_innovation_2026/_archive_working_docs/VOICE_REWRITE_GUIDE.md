```
Competitiveness:

Right now, I compete against three types of competitors:

1. Commodity scale farms (like ones in California) that sell bulk salad greens at $0.99/
container. They're cheaper but their product turns over so fast we lose customers to 
them in winter, and quality is inconsistent.

2. Boutique farms 30+ miles away that focus on farmers markets too. They have the same

3. Grocery stores that buy from regional distributors. They want consistent supply,

My competitive advantage is:
- Quality: our greens are harvested same-day and packed fresh (no overnight shipping)
- Reliability: 95%+ CSA fulfillment rate (we almost never miss a week)
- Transparency: customers know exactly where their food comes from

But I've been losing customers to out-of-state suppliers from October through March 
because I can't produce year-round. And I'm losing wholesale opportunities because I 
can't transplant fast enough or invest time in cost transparency (I'm guessing at what 
crops are actually profitable).

This application fixes both:
- Greenhouse automation extends my season from 8 to 11 months. That means I can serve 
  restaurants year-round and give CSA members winter salads they can't get elsewhere.
- Transplanting equipment + tracking system means I can scale salad production from 
  50 CSA members to 300 CSA members (we get 8 requests per year for this) AND offer 
  wholesale at prices that work for me because I'll know my actual costs.

Result: I compete on quality + reliability + price, not just hope.
```

**CURRENT (jargon):**

```
Environmental Stewardship: I farm certified organic land and I take soil health 
seriously. Precision irrigation delivers water only when and where sensors say it is 
needed — eliminating the overwatering that comes with manual irrigation. Less excess 
water means less nutrient leaching from my organic fields into local waterways.
```

**REWRITE (personal values):**
```
Environmental Stewardship:

I farm organic because I believe food production should improve the soil, not deplete it. 
That's not just environmentally responsible—it's the only way small farms stay viable 
long-term.

Right now, I overwater. When I manually turn on the pump, I water the entire field 
whether it needs it or not. That wastes water (which is increasingly expensive) and 
leaches nitrogen into local streams. I hate it, but I don't have another option.

Soil moisture sensors change that. They tell me exactly which parts of the field need 
water today and which parts are fine. Result: less excess water, less nutrient runoff, 
lower water bills, healthier soil.

More importantly: I want to farm in a way that my children (or whoever farms this land 
after me) can look at the soil and see that I improved it, not mined it. Precision 
irrigation is part of that. So is immediate cover cropping after harvest. The drop 
spreader lets me seed cover crop the same day a bed comes off—no bare soil, no erosion, 
continuous soil improvement.

Organic farming isn't just about avoiding chemicals. It's about building a system that 
works with the land instead of against it. This equipment lets me do that at scale.
```
