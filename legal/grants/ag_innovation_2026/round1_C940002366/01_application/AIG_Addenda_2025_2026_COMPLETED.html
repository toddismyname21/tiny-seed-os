1. The intent of this program is to support the competitiveness, resiliency, environmental stewardship, and vitality of Pennsylvania farmers and foresters. How will this project address these focus areas?


Competitiveness:

I compete with much larger operations for wholesale accounts and farmers market customers. My advantage is quality and local presence, but my disadvantage has been efficiency — I spend too many hours on manual irrigation and hand-transplanting. Smart irrigation, precision transplanting, and greenhouse automation directly bring my costs down, which means I can maintain organic and local premiums while still being competitive on price. The Checchi and Magli transplanter alone replaces hours of dibbling and hand-transplanting labor with precision mechanical placement at 2,500 plants per hour — and its 11-inch minimum row spacing is the only way I can mechanically transplant our 4-row Salanova beds at the density my crop plan requires. This will allow us to scale without hugely increasing labor costs and standardize planting across our operation. Greenhouse automation lets me produce year-round, so I am not losing customers to out-of-state suppliers every winter. Through the PA Department of Agriculture’s Farm Vitality Planning Grant, we are also developing our brand and product lines to reach a wider market — and organic certification differentiates our product in that larger marketplace. But branding only works if you can deliver consistent supply, and that is exactly what this equipment makes possible. We are actively developing branded clamshell salad greens for grocery retail, so we are not relying on just two sales channels. Salad greens are the foundation of my business, and every piece of this project makes that foundation stronger. Our CSA program is on a trajectory from 100 to 400 members, and consistent supply is what is holding us back — every week we cannot produce is a week we lose members to competitors.

Resiliency:

Water is the single biggest variable I cannot control. The Toro Tempus Ag system with soil moisture sensors and automated valve control reduces my vulnerability to drought and inconsistent rainfall — which is the primary crop risk for salad greens. We also strategically use shade cloth and watering to cool greens during the hottest parts of the season. When you are growing a crop that wilts in hours without water, you need a system that responds faster than a person walking fields. Greenhouse climate controls with motorized ventilation and shade cloth protect against weather variability and extend the reliable production window. The drop spreader enables immediate cover cropping after bed termination, which protects soil from erosion and builds organic matter — making the land more resilient season after season. This project also reduces my dependence on any single market channel by giving me the efficiency to serve wholesale, CSA, and direct-to-consumer simultaneously.

Environmental Stewardship:

I farm certified organic land and I take soil health seriously. Precision irrigation with the Tempus Ag system delivers water only when and where soil moisture sensors say it is needed — reducing excess water and preventing nutrient leaching from my organic fields into local waterways. The drop spreader is central to my soil health strategy — by seeding cover crop immediately after harvest, I eliminate bare soil periods that lead to erosion and nutrient loss. Banding fertilizer precisely along crop rows reduces total fertilizer use and prevents broadcast waste. The stainless steel rinse conveyor lets me efficiently sanitize and reuse harvest containers, reducing single-use material waste. Year-round local production in automated high tunnels reduces food miles for our Pittsburgh-area customers.

Vitality:

This project changes the kind of work available on my farm. Instead of seasonal manual labor, the positions I am building require technical skill — managing the Tempus Ag irrigation platform, operating a precision transplanter, running greenhouse climate systems. These are real jobs with a future, not just seasonal labor. Mechanized transplanting reduces the physical strain of hand-planting thousands of seedlings, which is some of the hardest work on any vegetable farm. I am a first-generation, beginning farmer building something from scratch, and I want Tiny Seed Farm to show that small-scale organic operations in Western Pennsylvania can integrate practical technology without losing what makes them special.


2. What metrics will you be able to track to show the success of this investment? Please list the anticipated outputs and outcomes that will be a result of this project.


Outputs

Water usage per bed-foot of salad greens — With the Tempus Ag flow meter and soil moisture sensors, I will establish a baseline in Month 1 and target a 25% reduction in water use by Year 2 compared to manual irrigation.

Transplanting rate (plants per hour) — The Checchi and Magli BABY COMPACT targets 2,500 plants per hour per operator, replacing current dibbling and hand-transplanting.

Greenhouse production months per year — I currently produce approximately 7 months per year. Greenhouse automation will extend production into the shoulder seasons on both ends.

Seedling tray seeding rate — The 200-cell drop seeders will allow us to seed complete trays in seconds instead of minutes.

Labor hours saved per week on irrigation — I currently spend 10+ hours per week on manual irrigation. The Tempus Ag system will reduce this to approximately 2 hours of monitoring per week.

Cover crop establishment rate — Every bed ends the season in cover. Target is 90% same-day turnaround by end of Year 1 via the drop spreader.

Wholesale and grocery clamshell sales — We are developing branded clamshell salad greens for grocery retail. We will track wholesale and clamshell sales as benchmarks, targeting a 30% year-over-year increase from Year 1 to Year 3. That is how we will know the production infrastructure is actually working.

Food safety compliance — With the rinse conveyor, I will track sanitation cycles and establish documented food safety protocols meeting FSMA requirements.

Jobs created or enhanced — This project will create 1 to 2 additional positions that require technical skill and provide career development opportunities.


Outcomes

Reduced operating costs per unit of production across all sales channels.

Year-round revenue stream, eliminating the 3 to 4 month winter gap.

Faster field turnover from harvest to cover crop to next planting, increasing successions per season.

Reduced water consumption per crop unit with documented data from the Tempus Ag platform.

Improved soil health through consistent cover cropping practices.

Improved food safety compliance at production scale.

Expansion into grocery retail through branded clamshell salad greens, so we are not dependent on just two sales channels.

CSA membership growth from 100 to 400 members as year-round supply improves.

A working example of how a small, diversified organic farm can build a complete production system with the support of state grant programs.


3. The Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture (PDA) has five innovation principles. How will this project demonstrate adherence to these principles?


1. Relevant

Every item in this proposal addresses a bottleneck I am dealing with right now. Water management, transplanting efficiency, season extension, and soil health are the challenges that come up in every conversation I have with other small-scale growers in Western PA. These are not theoretical problems — they are the reason small farms stay small. We have developed a reputation for our high quality salad greens, and we want to be able to serve more customers with the quality and flavor we are known for. The farms that can produce efficiently, year-round, at consistent quality are the ones that will survive.

2. Practical — Common Sense Solutions

All of this equipment integrates with what I already have and what I purchased through Round 1. The Toro Tempus Ag irrigation system uses LoRA wireless technology with a single base station covering 5,200 feet — I do not need an engineering degree to operate it. The AegisTEC greenhouse controllers are touchscreen units designed for growers, not technicians. The Checchi and Magli transplanter is a proven, commercially manufactured unit used by vegetable farms worldwide. The Schaper Brothers drop spreader is heavy-gauge galvanized steel built for daily farm use. None of this is experimental or laboratory-grade. It is practical equipment for a working farm.

3. Accessible — Tiny Seed Farm is all about sharing

Every component is commercially available and can be implemented at the individual farm level. I am not building custom technology that only works in my specific situation. The Tempus Ag platform, AegisTEC controllers, and all supporting equipment are sold through agricultural supply dealers — I am sourcing from Brookdale Farm Supplies and Nolt’s Greenhouse Supplies, both established vendors serving vegetable and greenhouse growers across the Northeast.

4. Scalable

The irrigation, transplanting, and greenhouse systems are designed to scale with production volume. The Tempus Ag platform supports additional base stations and zone controllers as acreage expands. A farm producing two or five times my current volume uses the same infrastructure with minimal additional investment. The greenhouse automation is modular — additional motors, shutters, and controllers can be added as structures are built. The drop spreader works on any 3-point hitch tractor. This is not equipment I will outgrow in two years. Any small vegetable farm in Pennsylvania facing these same bottlenecks could put together the same system from the same vendors.

5. Triple Bottom Line (People, Planet, Profit)

People — Technical positions that offer career development, not just seasonal work. Reduced physical strain from mechanized transplanting and automated irrigation. The kind of jobs people actually want to stay in.

Planet — Precision water use reducing runoff and waste. Immediate cover cropping protecting soil health after every harvest. Banded fertilizer reducing total input use. Year-round local production reducing food miles. Organic practices maintained at scale.

Profit — Lower per-unit costs across all channels. Year-round revenue eliminating the winter gap. Data from the Tempus Ag platform informing pricing and allocation. Faster field turnover increasing revenue per acre per season.
