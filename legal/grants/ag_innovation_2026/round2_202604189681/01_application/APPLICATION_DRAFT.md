## PROJECT NARRATIVE (15 pts)

Tiny Seed Farm is a first-generation, beginning organic vegetable operation in Rochester, PA (Beaver County), leasing 8 acres of certified organic farmland, greenhouses, and high tunnels. The farm serves wholesale restaurant accounts, a growing CSA program, and four Pittsburgh-region farmers markets.

In 2024, Tiny Seed Farm received a PA Agriculture Innovation Grant (#13, Application #202411157573, $75,000) to invest in precision seeding (Ortomec multi-seed), mechanical harvesting (Harvester 2000), composting infrastructure (PTO compost turner), and cultivation systems (Tilmor). This equipment addresses the core production pipeline from planting through harvest.

This Round 2 application completes the system — addressing the critical gaps that remain between the equipment already funded and a fully integrated, efficient seed-to-sale operation. Specifically:

**Gap 1: Water Management.** The farm currently relies on manual irrigation with no moisture monitoring. As production scales with new seeding and harvesting equipment, water management becomes the bottleneck. A smart irrigation system with remote pump and valve controllers, water-efficient drip/sprinkler infrastructure, and real-time soil moisture sensors will optimize water use, reduce labor, and protect crop quality — especially for salad greens where consistent moisture is critical for harvest quality.

**Gap 2: Post-Harvest Processing.** The Round 1 investment dramatically increases harvest volume, but post-harvest handling has not been upgraded to match. A stainless steel rinse conveyor and bin sanitization system will maintain food safety standards at higher volumes. A mechanized bagging machine will enable efficient packaging for wholesale and CSA distribution. These investments directly support the food safety and efficiency gains needed to scale from current production to the 300% salad greens increase projected in Round 1.

**Gap 3: Greenhouse Season Extension.** Greenhouse automation (climate controls, ventilation, heating management) will extend the growing season and stabilize production for year-round salad greens supply — reducing the seasonal revenue gap that is the single biggest financial challenge for the farm.

**Gap 4: Transplant System Integration.** The farm has invested in a new transplant system for salad production, but it requires a custom 3-point mounting system to adapt to current tractors. This integration is essential to connect the seeding/transplanting pipeline to the new cultivation and harvesting equipment from Round 1.

**Gap 5: Seed-to-Sale Tracking.** A hardware-based tracking system (barcode/RFID scanners, field-rated tablets/terminals, scale integration) will capture real-time data from planting through sale, feeding the Real Cost Accounting Tool that the farm is developing to quantify true production costs per crop. This data infrastructure supports precision decision-making and provides the metrics needed to report on both Round 1 and Round 2 outcomes.

Together with Round 1, these investments complete a FULL CIRCLE system: optimal seeding → precision cultivation → efficient harvest → automated post-harvest → smart water management → real-time tracking → data-driven decisions. Each component is meaningless in isolation; together they represent an integrated innovation that transforms a small organic farm into a scalable, data-driven operation.

## ADDENDA Q1: INTENT OF PROGRAM (30 pts)
*How does this project support competitiveness, resiliency, environmental stewardship, and vitality?*

**Competitiveness:**
Smart irrigation and post-harvest automation directly reduce per-unit production costs, allowing Tiny Seed Farm to compete with larger operations on price while maintaining organic and local premiums. The tracking system provides real-time cost visibility that enables dynamic pricing decisions across wholesale, CSA, and farmers market channels. Greenhouse automation enables year-round production, eliminating the seasonal gap that forces customers to source from out-of-state during winter months.

**Resiliency:**
Water management sensors and automated irrigation reduce vulnerability to drought and inconsistent rainfall — the primary crop risk for salad greens. Greenhouse climate controls protect against weather variability and extend the reliable production window. The tracking system captures operational data that enables rapid response to changing conditions: if a crop underperforms, the farmer knows in real time and can adjust planting, pricing, or allocation immediately rather than discovering the loss at season's end.

**Environmental Stewardship:**
Precision irrigation reduces water waste by delivering moisture only when and where sensors indicate need — eliminating the overwatering that is standard with manual irrigation. Reduced water runoff means less nutrient leaching from organic fields into waterways. The sanitization system enables safe reuse of harvest bins and equipment, reducing single-use material waste. Data from the tracking system quantifies the environmental benefit of organic practices in dollars — making the case for sustainable agriculture with hard numbers, not just philosophy.

**Vitality:**
This project creates equipment-operator positions that require technical skill and offer career development — not seasonal manual labor. Post-harvest automation reduces the physical strain of repetitive packaging work, improving working conditions. The farm serves as a model for how first-generation, beginning farmers can integrate technology into small-scale organic operations. The tracking system and Real Cost Accounting Tool will be documented as a replicable framework for other small farms in Western Pennsylvania.

## ADDENDA Q3: FIVE INNOVATION PRINCIPLES (10 pts)

**Relevant:** Every item in this proposal addresses a documented bottleneck in scaling small-farm organic salad greens production — the fastest-growing segment of local food demand in Western PA. Water management, post-harvest efficiency, and season extension are the three challenges most cited by small-scale growers as barriers to scaling.

**Practical:** All equipment integrates with existing farm infrastructure and Round 1 investments. The smart irrigation system uses standard valve controllers and soil sensors with wireless monitoring — no specialized training required. Post-harvest equipment is commercial-grade stainless steel designed for daily farm use, not laboratory conditions.

**Accessible:** Every component is commercially available and implementable at the individual farm level. The tracking system uses standard barcode/RFID technology adapted for agricultural use. The Real Cost Accounting methodology being developed will be documented and shared as an open framework for other small farms.

**Scalable:** The irrigation, post-harvest, and tracking systems are designed to scale with production volume. A farm producing 2x or 5x current volume uses the same infrastructure with minimal additional investment. The greenhouse automation is modular — additional zones can be added as the operation expands.

**Triple Bottom Line:**
- *People:* Technical positions replace manual labor; reduced physical strain; career pathways in ag-tech
- *Planet:* Precision water use, reduced runoff, sanitization reducing waste, year-round local production reducing food miles
- *Profit:* Lower per-unit costs, year-round revenue, data-driven pricing, reduced waste and spoilage
