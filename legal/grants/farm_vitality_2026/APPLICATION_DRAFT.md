## PROJECT DESCRIPTION (Narrative)

Tiny Seed Farm is a first-generation, beginning organic vegetable and cut flower operation in Rochester, PA (Beaver County), currently leasing approximately 8 acres of certified organic farmland, greenhouses, high tunnels, and equipment from a retiring farmer through the Donald J Kretschmann Revocable Trust. The farm is in its second full production year, serving wholesale restaurant accounts, a CSA program, and four farmers markets in the Pittsburgh region.

The farmland itself carries deep significance to Pennsylvania's agricultural heritage. Don Kretschmann was one of the earliest certified organic farmers in the Commonwealth, pioneering organic production methods in Western Pennsylvania at a time when few believed small-scale organic farming was viable. His decades of stewardship — building soil health, maintaining organic certification, and demonstrating that sustainable agriculture could work in this region — inspired a generation of farmers, including Todd Wilson, to believe that organic farming was something they could do. Ensuring that this land continues in active organic production under the next generation of stewardship is not just a business transaction — it is the continuation of a legacy that helped define organic agriculture in Pennsylvania.

As a young, first-generation farmer operating in a capital-intensive, thin-margin industry, Todd Wilson faces several interconnected challenges that this project will address through professional planning services, all in service of developing a **complete business plan** for the farming operation:

### Challenge 1: Lease Security and Legal Protection
The farm's current lease arrangement is a 1-year term with two 1-year renewals (3 years maximum). For a beginning farmer investing in soil health, perennial plantings, customer relationships, and organic certification continuity, this term is dangerously short. The lease requires professional legal review and negotiation to establish fair terms that protect both the landowner's property interests and the farmer's investment in improvements, with the goal of securing a longer-term arrangement that supports business planning and lender confidence.

Additionally, the lease contains provisions that require professional legal interpretation, including arrears from the prior year that need to be finalized, aesthetic and operational restrictions that could conflict with agricultural needs, and an absolute assignment prohibition that prevents standard business succession planning.

### Challenge 2: Financial Planning and Access to Capital
The farm needs professional financial review and guidance to validate cash flow projections, create lender-ready financial statements, and ensure the farm's budget accounts for lease costs (~$32,000+/year), early-season expenses, and expansion investments. The farm has established internal accounting systems using QuickBooks and proprietary farm management software (Tiny Seed OS) that track sales, crop costs, and operational data — but this internal work needs professional CPA review and validation to meet lender standards. This financial foundation is essential for securing working capital financing (FSA loans, operating lines of credit) to cover the gap between spring planting costs and summer/fall revenue.

### Challenge 3: Market Expansion to Support Innovation Grant Investment
Tiny Seed Farm has been awarded a PA Agricultural Innovation Grant (Application #202411157573, $75,000 PDA award) to invest in precision seeding equipment, mechanical harvesting systems, composting infrastructure, and cultivation tools that will dramatically scale the farm's salad greens production capacity. This equipment investment will significantly increase the farm's output — but production capacity without market development is wasted investment.

Professional marketing strategy is critical to develop the sales channels, pricing, branding, and customer acquisition needed to sell what this new equipment enables the farm to produce. The marketing plan funded by this project will ensure the state's Innovation Grant investment translates into actual revenue growth by expanding CSA membership (target: 100 to 400 members), developing wholesale partnerships, building the farm's brand identity, and creating a go-to-market strategy for scaled production. Without this planning, the farm risks overproduction without the market infrastructure to absorb it.

**End Goal:** A complete, professional business plan that integrates legal protection (secure lease), financial viability (bankable projections), and market strategy (growth roadmap aligned with Innovation Grant capacity) — giving Tiny Seed Farm the foundation to transition from a beginning operation to a sustainable, expanding business.

This project aligns with the following eligible purposes under the Farm Vitality Planning Grant Program:

1. **Creating business plans and management strategies to enhance farm viability** — comprehensive business plan integrating financial, legal, and marketing components
2. **Planning for the transition of ownership and operation of a farm to new owners and operators** — lease negotiation facilitates the transition of one of Pennsylvania's earliest certified organic farms from a retiring pioneer to a beginning farmer, ensuring continuity of decades of organic stewardship

4. **Maintaining the viability of farms with agricultural conservation easements** — the leased property includes a conservation easement; professional planning ensures the farm operation remains viable within those constraints
