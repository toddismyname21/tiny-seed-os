Crew-facing PRINTED sheets (pack sheets, pack-check, matrices, labels) must be designed to the farm's own readability research — not to information-density instincts.

**Why:** 2026-07-09, Todd: "We had done research to create a sheet that is easily read. The sheets you have been making have been pretty sprawling and challenging for the team to read — that is the feedback I have been getting." Multiple iterations (consolidated pack sheet, Pack House matrix) optimized for fewer pages / more data per page, and the crew found them hard to use. Dense ≠ readable for a gloved, hurried, mixed-language pack team.

**How to apply:** before building ANY crew-facing print surface, locate and follow the farm's print/UX research (docs/MOBILE_FARM_UX_RESEARCH.md, shared_research/ux_design_2026/CORE_UX_PRINCIPLES.md, and any pack-sheet-specific research docs — grep before designing). Concrete bias: LARGE type, few columns, one decision per line, strong section breaks, whitespace over density. Page count is NOT the objective — legibility at arm's length in a cold pack house is. When in doubt, prototype ONE page and get Todd's yes before rolling the pattern across surfaces. Related: [[holistic-systematic]].
