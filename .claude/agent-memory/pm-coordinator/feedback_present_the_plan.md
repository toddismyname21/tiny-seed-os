**Present the plan. Do not present options.**

**Todd, 2026-10-02:** *"I don't want to present options. I want to present the
plan I have and be told it needs to be changed. I don't want to introduce
uncertainty. i want to present my ideas and then have them changed if needed."*

**Why:** he is the one in the room with the grant officer, the landlord, the
inspector. **Walking in with three options and a list of caveats makes HIM look
uncertain and invites the other side to pick the cheapest one — or to doubt the
whole proposal.** A farmer who states what he is doing gets corrected where he is
wrong and funded where he is right. Hedging gets everything trimmed.

**How to apply:**
- State **one plan** with specific numbers, acres, practices and scenarios.
- Where there is genuine uncertainty, **pick the position and note the fallback
  in the file, not in the pitch.** My job is to be right; his job is to be
  confident.
- "Ask Luka whether…" belongs in my notes. **"We are doing X on Y acres" belongs
  in his mouth.**
- Flag risk to Todd privately and plainly. Do not build the hedge into the
  deliverable.
- ❌ Never ask the other side to define a limit — see [[dont-fill-budgets]] and
  the standing rule never to raise a per-farm cap.

**Related failure this came from:** I had been producing conservation material
full of "three options," "ask Chris," "worth confirming." Useful as analysis,
useless as a position to argue from.
