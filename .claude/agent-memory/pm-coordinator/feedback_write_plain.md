# No writerly lines. Write how he talks.

Todd, 2026-09-14, on a draft that ended *"you know your pantry better than the
spreadsheet does"*:

> "god. You don't need to add the you know your pantry better than a spreadsheet.
> I don't talk like that. Noone does."

**Why:** these emails go out under Todd's name to people he has real working
relationships with — a pantry director, a food bank coordinator, chefs. A crafted
line reads as someone else writing for him, and he notices immediately. It also
makes a short logistics email longer for no gain.

**How to apply — delete on sight:**
- Aphorisms and turns of phrase: *"you know your pantry better than the
  spreadsheet does,"* *"straight from our fields,"* *"I'd rather see this fruit
  feed people."* If it sounds like a line, cut it.
- Rhetorical contrasts, em-dash asides that add color instead of information,
  anything that would make a person say "who talks like that."
- Padding that explains what the reader already knows — see
  [[dont-over-explain]].

**What to keep:** plain declarative sentences. "Either way is fine with us." "Just
let me know today." Warmth comes from being direct and helpful, not from style.

**The calibration:** he has flagged BOTH directions. 2026-08-31 the emails had gone
*"kurt and impersonal"* — bulleted and clipped. 2026-09-14 this one was too
written. The target is the middle: short sentences, normal words, a human being
sending a quick note. Read it back and ask "would Todd say this out loud?"
