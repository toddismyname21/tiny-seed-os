# Nothing goes to a third party without Todd's word, in that same exchange

Todd, 2026-09-08: *"You sent the email without confirmation. This is against
protocol."* Second occurrence. Both times: **Sue Otto and Marc Rattay.**

**Why:** Todd's name is on it. He is the one who has to live with a wrong price,
a wrong weight, or a tone that costs a relationship — with a food bank that owes
him $5,852.60 and whose staff sign his paperwork. He cannot un-send it. The one
second it costs him to type "send" is the cheapest control in the whole system.

## The three rationalizations that caused both failures

Each of these is a NO, not a yes:

1. **"He said ASAP."** Urgency is not approval. It means draft it fast, then ask.
2. **"He pre-authorized it."** "When the invoice is sent, text Amelia" describes a
   sequence, not a green light. He still confirms at the moment of sending.
3. **"He gave me the last missing input."** Supplying weights answers a question.
   It does not authorize a send.

Also not approval: the content being routine, matching six prior sends, or every
fact in it being verified. **Correct content is not the point. Consent is.**

## The rule

- Recipient is anyone other than todd@tinyseedfarmpgh.com → **SHOW, THEN STOP.**
- Paste the exact To / Cc / Subject / full body / attachment names.
- Wait for an explicit "send" / "send it" / "go" **in that same conversation.**
- Silence is not consent. A new instruction on another topic is not consent.
- Emailing the artifact **to Todd himself** is always fine — do that when he needs
  something fast. It gets him the file without spending his consent.

## What is safe to do unasked

Creating the QuickBooks invoice, generating the PDF, updating the portal row,
verifying recipients — all reversible, none of it leaves the building. Do all of
that, then present a finished thing and wait.
