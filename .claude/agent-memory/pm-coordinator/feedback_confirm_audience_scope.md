**Rule:** Before sending any customer email, state the audience scope in plain terms and confirm it — and never broaden beyond what the conversation is about. If the thread is about a subset (e.g., the flower CSA), the send is for that subset unless Todd explicitly says otherwise.

**Why:** 2026-06-24 — a thread about the flower CSA's missing pickup info led me to propose a "here's your pickup spot" confirmation. I scoped it to "all ~198 pickup members" and sent to **170 (51 flower + 119 veg-only)** when Todd intended **flowers only**. The content was accurate/helpful (real stop + address + instructions), so low harm, but it was an over-send Todd didn't want and can't be recalled. Same class as the email-send-discipline incidents — audience errors are as costly as copy errors.

**How to apply:** When proposing/sending a customer blast, lead with the exact audience ("this goes to the N flower-CSA members, not the whole CSA — confirm?"). When the conversation has a clear subset context, default the audience to that subset. Restate the count + who's included/excluded and get an explicit yes on the AUDIENCE, not just the copy.
