For any customer-facing copy (CSA emails, SMS, wholesale comms, landing copy, etc.), drop ALL self-referential framing:

❌ DON'T write: "We rebuilt your portal." "Big news!" "Excited to announce." "Our new system..." — customers don't care about us, our process, our work.

✅ DO write: 3-part structure — every time.
1. **What it does for THEM** (the concrete benefits, in their language)
2. **HOW they do it** (the literal steps, brief)
3. **WHY it's good for ALL of us** (the mutual benefit — less waste, more food, fewer back-and-forth emails, more time)

**Why:** Todd's explicit direction 2026-06-02 reviewing CSA portal launch email drafts: "We don't need to explain that we built it. Who cares? Just tell them what it does for them and how to do it. And why it is good for all of us. The rest is not relevent."

**How to apply:** When drafting customer copy, write the first draft, then ruthlessly delete every sentence that's about US (the farm side). Test: would the customer care about this paragraph if it were about a competitor? If no — cut it. Keep only what serves them and the relationship.

**Standard signature** (Todd's 2026-06-02 direction) for all customer-facing emails/letters/SMS where space allows:

> — Farmer Todd and the Tiny Seed Crew

NOT plain "— Todd" or "Todd Wilson" or "The Tiny Seed Farm Team." Use the warm-farmer + inclusive-crew framing exactly as written.

**No prices to PREPAID members (Todd 2026-06-08):** In copy to members who already prepaid (CSA box members — the "everyone"/box-member emails), do NOT show share prices ($35/$45 etc.). "They prepaid. I don't want them to think about money." EXCEPTION: Flex members who are actively choosing/spending their balance DO need prices (they're shopping) — keep prices in flex-ordering copy. Rule of thumb: money appears only where the member is actively transacting; never in relationship/box-content updates.
