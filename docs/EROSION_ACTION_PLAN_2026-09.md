# Erosion Emergency Action Plan — September 2026

**Farm:** Tiny Seed Farm, 257 Zeigler Rd, Rochester PA 15074 (Beaver County, zone 6b)
**Event:** Sep 3, 2026 — ~2–2.5″ rain in under an hour on freshly tilled sloped fields,
after the wettest August on record (7.62″ at PIT, +4.10″ above normal). Rills, gullies,
topsoil loss.
**Written:** 2026-09-10. Mid-September is near-ideal emergency cover-crop timing in
western PA — speed matters more than perfection. Every day bare is another storm's worth
of soil.

---

## 1. DO TODAY

**A. Walk and triage (1 hour).** Map three zones: (1) sheet/rill erosion, (2) actual
gullies / concentrated-flow channels, (3) deposition at the slope bottom. **Photograph
everything** — needed for NRCS/district cost-share and the Ag E&S plan file. Log in
`FARM_JOURNAL.md`.

**B. Buy seed and straw today.** Call the co-op/seed dealer for:
- **Cereal (winter) rye** — the workhorse. Most cold-tolerant cover crop; germinates at
  soil temps down to ~34°F; can be seeded later than anything else. Drill 60–120 lb/ac;
  broadcast 90–160 lb/ac. For emergency erosion cover go high: **~120–160 lb/ac (2–3 bu)
  broadcast**.
- **Oats** (for beds you'll plant early next spring) — sow **this week**: ~2–3 bu/ac
  drilled (64–96 lb), 90–100+ lb/ac broadcast. Winterkills → protective mat + clean
  early-spring beds.
- **Straw:** ~**1.5–2 tons/ac** (~70–100 small squares/acre) for repaired areas and
  steep sections.

**C. Broadcast rye on every bare reachable area — before the next rain.** Overseeding
just ahead of rain is one of the most reliable establishment windows. Broadcast, then
lightly rake/harrow/cultipack for seed-soil contact; where you can't incorporate, straw
over the seed anchors it.

**D. Stop water at the top of the slope.** If runoff enters from above, cut a shallow
temporary diversion furrow or stake a line of straw bales/wattles along the top edge;
route water to a stable grassed outlet — never toward a neighbor or down a gully.

**E. Do NOT re-till the whole field to "clean up."** That resets the erosion clock.
Disturb only what's needed to fill gullies and make a seedbed.

## 2. THIS WEEK

**A. Repair rills and gullies, in order:**
1. **Fill and compact** — pull deposited topsoil from the slope bottom back into the
   channels and compact it (loose fill washes out in the first storm).
2. **Seed heavily** with rye (top-end rate).
3. **Mulch/blanket:** straw 1.5–2 tons/ac (right rate ≈ ~50% soil still visible; >3
   tons/ac smothers seedlings). Seed + 2 tons/ac mulch can cut erosion ~99% vs bare.
   Anchor straw on slopes (crimp, netting, or wet down). Use **erosion-control
   blankets** on repaired gully channels and slopes steeper than ~3:1 — fill/compact
   first, trench in the top edge.

**B. Break up slope length: straw wattles / silt socks on contour.** 9–12″ tubes,
trenched 2–3″, staked every 3–4 ft. Spacing: ~every 30 ft on a gentle 4:1 slope down to
~10 ft on 2:1. Staked straw-bale rows are the cheap substitute.

**C. Check dams** in any channel still carrying water (rock, fiber log, sandbag, or
compost sock) until vegetation takes; set the upstream dam's toe level with the
downstream dam's crest.

**D. Make two calls (details §5):**
- **Beaver County Conservation District — (724) 378-1701** (Ag E&S plan help + site visit)
- **NRCS Butler field office (serves Beaver Co.) — (724) 482-5297** (EQIP: cover crop
  340, grassed waterway 412, diversion 362)

**E. Overseed standing fall crops** with rye (~45–90 lb/ac) or oats (~60 lb/ac) just
before rain so no bed is bare after harvest.

## 4. NEXT SEASON REDESIGN (2027)

**Todd's strip-till idea: YES — with two modifications.**
- Strip tillage (till only the ~8–12″ planting zone, covered soil between) is a proven
  erosion-reducing system for vegetables, including small farms (Cornell, UMN, OSU).
- **Mod 1 — orientation:** strips must run **on the contour, across the slope** — never
  up-and-down. Alternate tilled strips with cover strips.
- **Mod 2 — the timing rule:** this year's failure mode wasn't tillage per se, it was
  tilled ground sitting bare through a storm. Standing rule: **never till more than you
  will plant or cover within a few days; nothing bare going into forecast rain.**

**Bed-by-bed cover cropping: also yes — the complementary half.** Cover-crop in bed-width
strips alternating with cash beds; permanent clover living pathways between permanent
beds; interseed covers into standing late-summer crops so beds are protected the day the
crop comes out.

**Tarping = the erosion-safe version of "incrementally uncover."** Kill cover/weeds under
5–6 mil silage tarp 3–6 weeks, peel back one bed at a time, plant into near-undisturbed
soil (UMass/UMaine/Cornell). No steel needed for small acreage.

**Recommended system:**
1. Permanent raised beds **on contour**, permanent clover/fescue pathways.
2. NRCS-designed **grassed waterway** in the natural drainage line.
3. Every bed is always in **cash crop, cover crop, or under tarp — never bare**. Retire
   whole-field tillage.
4. **Contour cover strips** every 50–100 ft of slope length (doubles as the rotation's
   cover block).
5. Till/peel tarps max a few days before planting, seed in hand; every bed gets rye or
   oats by the date table above each fall.
6. Deep compost mulch on highest-value permanent beds.

## 6. LEGAL — PA Ag E&S Plan (25 Pa. Code Ch. 102)

- Any agricultural plowing/tilling disturbing **≥5,000 sq ft** (0.11 ac — i.e., us)
  requires a **written Ag Erosion & Sediment Control Plan**, implemented and kept on the
  farm (§102.4(a)). Below that, BMPs are still required. Landowner is responsible.
- No filing/permit needed — but visible sediment leaving the property (like this event)
  is what triggers complaints and DEP/district enforcement.
- **Action:** ask BCCD this week to help develop/update the Ag E&S plan and document the
  remediation already underway. Plan writing is ~75% REAP-creditable.
