# Print labels in house? — the Epson ColorWorks C4000

**Todd 2026-10-02:** *"What about the idea of printing our own labels? …we don't
have solidified designs and until we do this might be the best solution?"*
And: *"let's remember that these products will be refrigerated and must be able
to stand moisture."*

---

# 1. 🔴 FIRST — you linked the WRONG MODEL

You sent the **Matte** version (`C31CK03A9981`). Epson's own product page says:

> *"The C4000 **matte** printer comes with black ink that delivers a darker black
> on matte and plain paper label media, compared to the gloss black ink.
> **This ink is not recommended for printing on gloss label media.**"*

**Refrigerated product needs gloss poly (synthetic) media.** Paper labels wick
condensation, soften, and release. So the matte model is pointed at exactly the
media you cannot use.

➡️ **Buy the GLOSS model** — `C31CK03A9971`.

# 2. ✅ But the machine itself is right, and Epson proves it

Two statements from the same page settle the moisture question:

> *"delivering durable color labels using **pigment-based** Epson Genuine Ink"*

Pigment, not dye. Dye ink runs when wet; pigment does not.

> ⭐ *"ColorWorks CW-C4000 inkjet printer with **UltraChrome DL pigment ink**,
> used in combination with **Neenah Kimdura poly media**, has been **BS 5609
> certified**."*

**BS 5609 is the marine standard** — the label must survive **three months'
immersion in seawater** and remain legible and adhered. A walk-in cooler and
condensation on a bag of greens is far below that bar.

**So the combination that meets your requirement is specific:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Printer | **ColorWorks C4000, GLOSS** |
| Ink | **UltraChrome DL pigment** (SJIC41P), $39.00/cartridge × 4 = **$156/set**, 50 ml each |
| Media | **poly / BOPP synthetic — Neenah Kimdura is the Epson-certified one.** NOT paper |
| Adhesive | ⚠️ **specify wet-strength / all-temperature.** Labels go onto cold, damp bags — a standard permanent adhesive will not grab |

🔴 **The adhesive is the part most likely to be got wrong**, and it is not on
Epson's page. It must be specified when the stock is ordered.

# 3. Why in-house beats pre-printed HERE — and it is not mainly cost

| | |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Designs are not settled** | Todd said so. Pre-printing locks them in |
| ⭐ **OEFFA may require label approval before use** | Committing 35,000 pre-printed labels before the certifier signs off risks eating the lot |
| ⭐ **SKU count is moving** | It went from 4 to **7 in one afternoon** — Cherry Tomato, Baby Arugula, Something Fresh Mix, Petite Kale Mix, Wild Arugula, Baby Spinach, **King Spring Mix**. Every added SKU is another pre-print run and another setup charge |
| **No minimum per SKU** | King Spring Mix is the most popular; Wild Arugula may be a tenth of it. Pre-printing forces the same minimum on both |
| ⭐ **Variable data** | Print **lot code and pack date onto the label itself** — a GAP traceability requirement met by equipment rather than a binder. The band sealer already codes; this puts it on the label |
| Seasonality | print what the week actually needs |

# 5. RECOMMENDATION

1. ✅ **Switch the model to GLOSS.** This is not negotiable given refrigeration.
2. ✅ **Specify poly/BOPP media with a wet-strength, all-temperature adhesive.**
   Paper will fail.
3. **Get a reseller quote** rather than buying from a 1-review Amazon listing —
   and ask them for realistic cost per label at your size.
4. **Ask Chris whether the flail mower can sit on the Conservation side.** That
   single answer decides whether the printer fits.
5. ⭐ **In the meantime, outsource ONE run of King Spring Mix** — it is the most
   popular, ~$131 for 1,000, and it tests the design, the adhesive and the
   cooler in the real world **before** $2,868 is committed to a printer or
   35,000 labels are committed to a design.

> **Point 5 is the one I would do this week.** It is the cheapest possible way to
> find out whether a label survives your cooler.
