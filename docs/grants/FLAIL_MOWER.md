## The justification to write in

> **48" flail mower — to cut crop residue and cover crops and return the material
> to the soil in place on FSA farm 1068/446**, where our salad greens and
> tomatoes are grown. Flail mowing shreds residue finely so it breaks down
> rapidly rather than lying in mats, feeding soil organic matter directly on the
> beds that produce both commodities. This supports our residue-management and
> soil-building practice and reduces the labor and tractor passes required to
> turn a bed between successions — the constraint on how many successions of
> salad greens we can run in a season, and therefore on how many weeks we can
> supply a wholesale buyer.

That last clause is the one doing the work. **Market access is the test for
Business Development**, so the argument has to end at supply, not at soil.

## ⚠️ Expect pushback — and know the answer

Pasa's eligibility rule 1: *"Is this a conservation practice? If so, the
practice should be funded through **Conservation Practice Implementation**."*

Residue management is **NRCS 329 / 345**, and soil carbon amendment is
**336/808** — both on Pasa's own practice list. So they may well say this
belongs in the conservation bucket.

**If they do, that is a WIN, not a loss.** It means the mower comes out of
conservation money and **the full amount stays in Business Development** for
post-harvest.

**The counter-argument if they resist entirely** — their own Overview gives a
mower as the example of an eligible BD item:

> *"General items under $10k must have a specific use connected to a practice.
> **For example purchasing a mower for mechanical weed control in an organic
> agroforestry system.**"*

A mower tied to a practice is explicitly contemplated. Ours is tied to residue
management on the two named commodities and the named field.
