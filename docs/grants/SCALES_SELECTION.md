### Why this one — the resolution matters at ¼ lb

| Scale | Resolution | % of a **¼ lb** bag | % of 1 lb |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brecknell 6030** | **0.002 lb** | **0.8%** | 0.2% |
| VisionTechShop DSW-100 | 0.01 lb | 4.0% | 1.0% |
| VisionTechShop TVS | 0.01 lb | 4.0% | 1.0% |

Your ¼ lb bag is the tightest tolerance you pack. At 0.01 lb resolution the
scale can only see in 4% steps of that bag — which means either short bags or,
more likely, **giving product away on every one.** At 0.002 lb it sees in 0.8%
steps.

**IP67 matters too.** This is a wet pack house with greens coming off a spinner.
A sealed scale survives; a splash-resistant one eventually does not.

**NSF supports the GAP argument** — food-contact equipment with a recognized
sanitation certification.

10 lb capacity covers the 3 lb bag with better than 3× headroom.

## ⚠️ One thing to check: does Pennsylvania require NTEP?

**NTEP** is "legal for trade" certification. It is generally required for scales
used to determine price **at the point of sale**. For **pre-packaged** goods —
which is what we are doing, filling bags ahead of sale — the usual requirement is
that the stated **net weight is accurate** (NIST Handbook 133), not that the
packing scale itself is NTEP.

**But the Brecknell 6030 is NSF, not NTEP.** If PA Weights & Measures wants NTEP
on a packing scale, the cheapest NTEP option jumps to roughly $675–1,264 each.

- [ ] **Call PA Weights & Measures** before ordering — Department of Agriculture,
      Bureau of Ride & Measurement Standards. One phone call, and it is the
      difference between $298 and $2,529 for the pair.

If NTEP is required, the budget absorbs it: there is **$4,777 unallocated**.
