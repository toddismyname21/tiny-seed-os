### Two cautions

⚠️ **Must be laid out on the true contour by a planner with a level**, not by eye.
Off-contour strips concentrate water instead of slowing it.

⚠️ **Permanent, and it changes the field geometry forever.** The beds are long and
machine-worked; contour strips cut across that. **Same tension as alley cropping
with the precision cultivation system AIG funded**, though less severe.


**What it is:** strips of permanent vegetation running **across the slope, on the
contour**, between cropped strips. They break the slope length so water never
builds enough speed to carry soil.

⭐ **The "Specialty Crops" scenario pays $2,203.40/ac** — the highest per-acre
rate relevant to this farm, because the foregone income from vegetable ground is
valued far above commodity ground.

**Where here:** **FSA Field 1 (1068/446/1)** — 33.55 acres of HEL, the largest
piece of cropland on the operation.

**What we do:** a conservation planner lays out the contour lines; strips are
seeded to permanent grass; cropping continues between them. ⚠️ **This must be
laid out properly — on the contour, not by eye. That is what the TAP is for.**

## 412 — Grassed Waterway

Only two scenarios. **"With checks" pays $982/ac more** — checks are small

disturbance** and the CPA-52 will scrutinise them; the plain scenario at $2,957

**One of the highest per-acre rates on the entire list** — behind only Riparian
Buffer (closed, no stream) and Contour Buffer Strips at $2,203.

### 🔴 THE UNANSWERED QUESTION

**Where does water concentrate on this ground after a heavy rain?**

Not in any record I have. It is something Todd has watched happen and almost
certainly never written down.

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| there is a drainage line / swale through or below cropped ground that washes | ⭐ **near the top of the list** — $1,500–$3,000 realistically, since most waterways here would be well under an acre |
| the ground sheds evenly with no concentrated flow | ❌ **412 does not apply. Drop it.** |

**On HEL ground concentrated flow is the mechanism that actually moves soil** —
sheet erosion is slow, but a gully takes a bed out in one storm.

➡️ **ASK TODD: after a big storm, where does the water run?**


**What it is:** a shaped, grassed channel following the natural drainage line, so
concentrated water flows over grass instead of cutting a gully through the field.

**Where here:** wherever water concentrates after a heavy rain. ⚠️ **I do not
know where that is.** Ask on the site visit — *"where does the water run after a
big storm?"* Every farmer knows; it rarely gets written down.

**What we do:** shape the channel, seed to sod-forming grass, maintain it, and
never crop through it.

🔴 **"With checks" means structures** — rock or similar across the channel to slow
flow. **That is soil disturbance**, so the CPA-52 will scrutinise it.
