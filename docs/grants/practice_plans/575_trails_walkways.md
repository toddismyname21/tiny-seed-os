## 1. What it is

A constructed path for people and equipment that stops traffic from compacting,
rutting and eroding the soil — and keeps mud out of where it should not be.

## ⭐ 2. Why this one is better than it sounds

**Mud between the field and the wash station is a daily, unglamorous, genuinely
expensive problem.** It compacts soil, it tracks into the pack house, and on a
farm that just invested in a chilled water loop and a food safety plan, **mud is
a food-safety issue, not just an annoyance.**

**It is also exactly the kind of thing a farm budget never covers.** It never
competes with seed or a tractor repair, so it never gets done.

## 3. What we physically do

| | |
|---|---|
| Identify the routes | field-to-pack-house, around the wash station, the paths that turn to soup |
| Build | geotextile fabric, then rock or gravel on top |
| Width | wide enough for a cart or the Mule, not just a person |

**Where:** the ground between the production blocks and the wash/pack area, and
around the pack house itself.

## 4. 🔴 The food safety connection — use this

The written food safety plan (10 owner hours in the BD budget) will have a
section on **traffic patterns and preventing contamination from entering the
pack area.** A constructed walkway is a direct, physical answer to that.

➡️ **Propose 575 and the food safety plan together.** Each justifies the other.

## 6. Practical note

⚠️ **Leased land** — gravel on a path is far less permanent than concrete, but
Don should still know.

⚠️ **The $3.00/sq ft cap is tight.** A 4-ft wide, 300-ft walkway is 1,200 sq ft,
so capped at $3,600. Price the stone and fabric before assuming the base payment
covers it.
