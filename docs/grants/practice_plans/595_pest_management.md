## 1. What it is

**PAMS = Prevention · Avoidance · Monitoring · Suppression.** It is a systems
approach to pest management where chemical control is the last step, not the
first. **For a certified organic farm this is not a new way of working — it is
the only way of working.** The practice pays you to formalise and document it.

## 2. What we physically do

| PAMS element | What it is here |
|---|---|
| **Prevention** | crop rotation, clean transplants, sanitation of trays and the pack house, removing crop residue promptly |
| **Avoidance** | **insect netting** over brassicas and salad greens, timing plantings around pest flights, variety selection |
| **Monitoring** | ⭐ **scouting on a schedule**, with written counts and thresholds — this is the part that must be documented |
| **Suppression** | beneficial habitat, OMRI-listed materials only when thresholds are crossed |

**Where:** salad blocks in **FSA Field 1** (flea beetle on brassica greens is the
defining pest), tomatoes in **Field 2**.

## 4. 🔴 What we record

| Record | When |
|---|---|
| **Written pest management plan** — target pests, thresholds, what we do at each threshold | **before the season** |
| ⭐ **Scouting log** — date, block, pest, count, who scouted. **On a schedule, not when you notice a problem** | **weekly in season** |
| Netting log — on/off dates per block | each event |
| Action log — what was applied or done, when, why, at what threshold | each action |
| ⭐ **ADJUSTMENT** — *"flea beetle pressure in JS3 crossed threshold twice under netting, so we moved brassicas out of that block"* | whenever |

**The weekly scouting log is the backbone.** It is what distinguishes a system
from a reaction.
