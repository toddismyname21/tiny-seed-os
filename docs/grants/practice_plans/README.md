# Practice plans — how each one actually works on this farm

One file per practice. Each answers the same five questions:

1. **What is it, in plain English**
2. **What we physically do** — which field, when
3. **What we buy**
4. **What we record** — this is where the money is
5. **What it pays**

## The practical explanation first

### You are not buying things and getting paid back. You are being paid to farm a certain way.

That is the whole idea. NRCS has decided that certain practices are worth paying
for because they protect soil and water. They publish a **rate** for each one.
**You get the rate for doing the practice.** Your receipts are not the measure.

Think of it the way the organic premium works: you do not get paid more for
salad because certification cost you money. You get paid more because you farmed
a certain way and can prove it. **This is the same shape — the proof is the
paperwork.**

### Which is why records ARE the product

For the flat "Adaptive Management" practices, **the deliverable is not a purchase
— it is a documented management system.** Plan, do, measure, adjust, record.

**A farm that tills less and writes nothing down gets $0. A farm that tills less
and documents it gets $6,075.** The difference is the file, not the field.

### The money comes before the work

80% on contract signing. 20% on verification. $1,500 stipend yearly. No match.
**You are not fronting anything.**

### The one hard gate

🔴 **Nothing may be implemented before the CPA-52 environmental review is
approved.** Not tarps, not fence, not a bin. USDA's words: *"Producers cannot
implement practice before an Environmental Evaluation is performed."*
