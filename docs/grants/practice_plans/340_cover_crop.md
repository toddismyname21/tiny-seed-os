## 1. What it is

Growing a crop you do not harvest, to protect and build soil — under a written
plan with monitoring and documented adjustment.

## 2. What we physically do

Already happening. The change is the paperwork around it.

| Window | Typical move |
|---|---|
| **After the last summer cash crop** | drill or broadcast a winter cover — cereal rye, or rye + vetch |
| **Short gaps between successions** | fast cover — buckwheat in summer, oats in fall |
| **Spring termination** | **crimp, mow with the flail, or tarp** — not tillage, which also feeds 345/329 |

**Where:** across the salad blocks in **FSA Field 1**, plus the tomato ground in
**Field 2** after the crop comes out.

**Scale:** 6 acres of the production beds over a season, counting short covers.

## 3. What we buy

Cover crop seed. ~$55/ac is a reasonable working figure for rye or a simple mix;
**confirm against the actual Welter Seed order** rather than this estimate.

## 4. 🔴 What we record

| Record | When |
|---|---|
| **Cover crop plan** — species, seeding rate, dates, which beds, goal for each | **before the season** |
| Seeding log — date, bed, species, rate, method | each seeding |
| **Establishment check** — did it come up, how well, photo | 2–3 weeks after seeding |
| Termination log — date, method, how complete | each termination |
| **Soil observations** — organic matter trend from Logan Labs, structure, earthworms | annually |
| ⭐ **ADJUSTMENT** — *"oats winterkilled earlier than planned in the JS blocks, so we moved to rye there"* | whenever |

## 5. Practical notes

✅ **The organic scenario pays a premium** ($123.47 vs $92.33 conventional) — so
even if Adaptive Management is refused, ask for the **organic** per-acre
scenario, not the plain one.

⭐ **This stacks with 336 Soil Carbon Amendment.** Cover crops build organic
matter; compost adds it. Different practices, same goal, both payable.
