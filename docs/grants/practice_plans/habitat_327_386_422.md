⭐ **"Foregone income" is the key phrase.** These pay you for the production you
give up by not cropping that ground — which is why they suit **edges, corners and
awkward margins** rather than good beds.

**On a farm with 7.6 acres of intensive production inside 97 acres of tract,
there is a lot of edge.**

## 327 — Conservation Cover

**What it is:** permanent vegetative cover on land taken out of production.

**Where here:** field corners the tractor cannot turn in, the strip between
production and the woods, anything awkward. **Approved in Pasa's case study** for
pollinator habitat.

**What we do:** prepare, seed a native mix with pollinator species, mow once a
year at most, keep it.

## 386 — Field Border

**What it is:** a strip of permanent vegetation **at the edge of a cropped
field**.

**Where here:** the margins of **FSA Field 1** where the salad blocks end. On
HEL ground a vegetated border also catches sediment — it is erosion control that
happens to be habitat.

**What we do:** take a strip out of production, seed it to a pollinator mix,
maintain it.

## ⭐ 422 — Hedgerow Planting — "Beetle Bank"

**What a beetle bank is:** a raised strip, usually a metre or so wide, planted to
tussock-forming native grasses, running through or alongside cropped ground. It
houses **predatory ground beetles and spiders over winter**, which then move out
into the crop in spring and eat pest larvae.

⭐ **This is direct organic pest control that pays you to build it** — and it
feeds **practice 595 PAMS** as a suppression strategy. **Two practices reinforcing
each other, both payable.**

**Where here:** a strip through or beside the salad blocks in Field 1.

## Practical notes

⚠️ **Acreage must be real and mapped.** Foregone-income payments are on measured
ground, and a TAP will map it at the site visit.

⚠️ **Leased land** — taking ground out of production is a change Don should know
about, even on margins.

✅ **No conflict with organic certification.** Native plantings and beneficial
habitat are positively regarded in an OSP.
