## 1. What it is

Growing a planned sequence of crops on the same ground to break pest and disease
cycles, manage fertility, and vary rooting depth and residue.

## 2. What we physically do

**Already happening.** Any certified organic vegetable farm rotates — the NOP
requires it.

| | |
|---|---|
| The rotation | salad greens → fruiting crops → cover → back round, varying family |
| Why it works | brassica greens and solanaceous tomatoes are different families, so soilborne pressure does not build |

**Where:** across the production blocks in **FSA Fields 1 and 2.**

## 3. 🔴 What we record

**You already produce most of this for OEFFA.** The organic system plan requires
a rotation plan and field histories.

| Record | When |
|---|---|
| **Written rotation plan** — block by block, several years forward | annually |
| **Field history** — what grew in each block, each year | ⭐ *already required by NOP* |
| Observations — disease or pest pressure by block over time | in season |
| Adjustment — where the rotation changed and why | as it happens |

⭐ **This is the cheapest practice to qualify for on the entire list**, because
the records already exist for organic certification. **Ask Luka whether the OSP
