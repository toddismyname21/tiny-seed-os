## ✅ REVIEWED 2026-10-06 — Microjet is the scenario, for COOLING

**Todd: *"salad mix cooling is very important and micro emitters is probably just
what we need on the hottest of days."*** The standard backs this directly:

> *"...and **auxiliary water needs such as leaching, frost protection, and
> cooling**."* — CPS 441

**Cooling is a named water need inside the standard.** It does not have to be
argued in.

⭐ **This reverses my earlier advice.** I said avoid microjet because spray wets
salad foliage. On the hottest days **wetting the canopy is the point** —
evaporative cooling keeps greens from bolting and going bitter.

⭐ **And it links the whole project:** cooling the canopy pre-harvest means **less
field heat arriving at the wash station**, so the hydrocooler and chilled water
loop do less work. **Pre-harvest and post-harvest cooling are one system.**

### 🔴 Sumisansui — probably NOT fundable

> *"Use CPS Code **442, Sprinkler System**, for systems that **uniformly wet the
> entire field** and typically have design discharge rates of **60 gal/hr or
> greater** at individual application discharge points."*

**Sumisansui R-Wide is a layflat spray tube wetting the entire bed uniformly →
reads as 442.** ⚠️ **442 is NOT on Pasa's 36-practice list**, so it is likely not
fundable through AMP conservation.

✅ **Microjets stay in 441** — discrete emitters at 5–30 gal/hr, well under the 60
gal/hr threshold. The line is *discrete low-discharge emitters* vs *a tube wetting
everything*.

### What "Surface PE with Emitters" is

Heavier-wall polyethylene (½" or ⅝") laid on the surface with emitters built into
the line. Versus **drip tape** — thin-wall 4–15 mil, collapses when empty, largely
a single-season consumable. PE lasts many seasons, resists rodent and cultivation
damage, holds pressure over long runs. **That is why it pays $6,436 against tape's
$1,208.**

## 1. What it is

Delivering water directly to the root zone at low pressure and low volume — drip
tape, drip line with emitters, or microjets — instead of overhead.

## 2. What we physically do

Drip is already in use. This would fund **expansion, replacement, or an upgrade
to a better-designed system.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Surface PE with emitters** | heavier-wall polyethylene line with built-in emitters. Lasts years, unlike seasonal tape |
| **Microjet** | small spray emitters. Better suited to tomatoes and tunnels than to dense salad beds |
| Supporting | filtration, pressure regulation, zone valves, header layout |

**Where:** **FSA Field 1** salad blocks, **Field 2** tomatoes, and the tunnels.
