## 1. What it is

Establishing trees or shrubs and **keeping them alive** — the payment covers the
plant, the protection (tube, cage, mulch) and the establishment period.

## 2. What we physically do

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **Field margins and awkward corners** | ground the tractor cannot work anyway |
| **Alongside a beetle bank or hedgerow (422)** | stacks the two practices |
| **As a windbreak line** | shelters salad blocks from wind damage |
| **A small orchard** | the case-study farm planted fruit trees |

⭐ **Perennials are precisely what a vegetable farm never plants**, because they
pay nothing for three to seven years and the money always goes to this season's
crop. **This is the money that breaks that cycle.**

## 3. What we buy

## 4. 🔴 What we record

| Record | When |
|---|---|
| **Planting plan** — species, number, spacing, exact location | before |
| Planting log — date, species, count, stock type | at planting |
| ⭐ **Survival counts** — *this is what the practice actually pays for.* Count and record | **annually, for the establishment period** |
| Replacement log — what died, what was replanted | as needed |
| Photos, same spot, same angle | annually |

⭐ **Survival is the deliverable, not planting.** Plant 100 and lose 60 and the
practice has failed. Protection and water in years one and two are the whole job.

## 5. Practical notes

⚠️ **Leased land, long horizon.** Trees outlive leases. **Don must agree**, and
there should be a conversation about what happens to them if the lease ends.
⭐ *That said — a landlord usually sees fruit trees as an improvement, not a
problem. This may be an easier conversation than fence or concrete.*

⭐ **Species worth considering:** elderberry and aronia establish fast and have a
market; fruit trees for a U-pick or CSA add-on; native shrubs if the goal is
habitat for **595 PAMS**.
