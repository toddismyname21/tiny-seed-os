# 1. THE HEADLINE GAP

## AIG is 15 lines of steel and $0 of selling.

| | Lines | PDA money |
|---|---|---|
| AIG Round 1 (executed) | 7 | $75,000 |
| AIG Round 2 (awarded, lines TBC) | 8 requested | $31,380 |
| **Total** | **15** | **$106,380** |

Go down all fifteen: seeder, stone burier, cultivation tooling, compost turner,
harvester, harvest platform, humidity control, transplanter, greenhouse
automation, irrigation, rinse conveyor, irrigation delivery, drop spreader,
spray tubes, drop seeders.

**Every one is a machine that grows or moves a crop. Not one dollar goes to
brand, label, photograph, sign, line sheet, website, point of sale, or
certification.**

AIG made this farm able to **grow** far more salad. Nothing in it makes the farm
able to **sell** far more salad. PASA BD is the only money on the table that can,
and its own stated purpose — *"help farmers market their products, gain access to
new markets, and expand their current markets"* — is precisely the hole.

## And it is worse than a neutral gap — AIG actively widened the bottleneck.

An 18-line Seed Spider, a 4-row Tilmor cultivator and a Harvester 2000 raise
field throughput by a large multiple. Downstream of the harvest, the farm still
dumps greens into a 200-gallon stock tank and bags them in **three separate
touches — fill, tie, sticker.**

**AIG bought capacity to the harvest line and stopped.** That is not a criticism
of AIG; harvest equipment is what it funds. But it means the pack house is now
the binding constraint on everything AIG paid for, and relieving it is the
highest-return dollar on the farm.

⭐ **This is the narrative for the PASA application, and it is true rather than
spin:** a state program funded the production half of seed-to-sale. The farm can
now grow more greens than it can wash, cool, pack, label, and sell. BD closes
that half.

## One distinction worth having ready for Chris

AIG R1 #1 is a **humidity control system** for the walk-in. If anyone asks why
BD is also buying cooling, the answer is that these do different jobs:

| | Does what |
|---|---|
| **Humidity control** (AIG) | keeps already-cold greens from wilting **in storage** |
| **Forced-air cooling** (BD) | pulls **field heat out** on arrival, in minutes instead of hours |

A walk-in *holds* temperature. It takes hours to pull heat from the middle of a
packed tote, and **the first hours after harvest set shelf life.** They are
complementary, not duplicative — and the AIG humidity line has $0 spent anyway.

## 4.2 The double-dip rule is narrower than it looks — but check for FEDERAL programs

Pasa's exact wording, read today:

> *"Double Dipping — If you are working with another AMP grant or **federal
> program**, we cannot fund the same commodity, practice, or field as the other
> program."*

**AIG and FVPG are Pennsylvania state programs. Neither triggers this.** That
removes the conflict worry for all fifteen AIG lines.

But note the rule bites on **commodity, practice, OR field** — any one of the
three. So the question that matters is whether the farm is in any **federal**
program touching the greens/tomato fields:

| Program | Federal? | Need to check |
|---|---|---|
| NRCS **EQIP** contract | ✅ federal | 🔴 **ASK TODD — this is the real risk.** An EQIP high tunnel or mulching contract on the same field would collide head-on |
| NRCS **CSP** | ✅ federal | same |
| **OCCSP** organic cost share | ✅ federal pass-through | likely fine (certification, not field/practice) but disclose |
| FSA loans | ✅ federal | credit, not a practice — almost certainly fine |
| AIG, FVPG | ❌ PA state | ✅ clear |

➡️ **Action: Todd confirms whether any NRCS/EQIP or CSP contract is active on
farm 1068 / tract 446.** If one is, the conservation practice chosen for PASA

# 8. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION

Pennsylvania's AIG program committed **$106,380** to this farm across fifteen
line items, and every one of them is production equipment — seeding, field prep,
cultivation, irrigation, compost, harvest. It stops at the harvest line.
Downstream, salad greens are still dumped into a stock tank and bagged in three
separate touches, and **nothing in either AIG round dries, cools, weighs, bags,
seals, codes, labels, or markets anything.** AIG raised what this farm can grow
and left untouched what it can pack and sell, which makes the pack house the
binding constraint on the entire state investment. PASA's Business Development
money is the only funding available for that half of seed-to-sale, and closing it
is what turns $106,380 of machinery into actual sales.
