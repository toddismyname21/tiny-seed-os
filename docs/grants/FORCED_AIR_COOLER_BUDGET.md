# Forced-air coolers ×2 — budget

Built to the UVM Extension design:
`blog.uvm.edu/cwcallah/2018/10/09/construction-details-for-a-pallet-forced-air-cooler/`
Prices checked **2026-10-02**.

## What it is

A plywood-and-2×12 plenum box that sits against a pallet of product inside the
cooler you already own. A 12" blower draws air **through** the boxes instead of
letting it drift around them. Poly sheeting channels the air so it has nowhere
to go but through the product.

UVM's note after field trials: *"a smaller (shorter) unit works well. Many farms
aren't packing full 6' pallets, and we found that a **4' version** was
suitable."* **Build the 4' version** — less lumber, less plastic, fits our scale.

## Notes on the build

- **Plenum:** 43" wide × 74" tall × 11-1/4" deep from 2×12 — or 4' tall per
  UVM's revision.
- **Panel:** cut an **11-1/4" circle** for the blower inlet, **centered** — UVM
  is explicit that centering gives even air pressure.
- **Critical detail:** the air must be pulled **horizontally through from one end
  under SUCTION, not pressure.** That is the whole principle. Get it backwards
  and it does nothing.
- **Plastic:** UVM used duct tape first, then wrapped the poly around strapping
  and screwed it on with fender washers. They recommend **wiggle wire** as an
  upgrade so the plastic can be replaced easily. Worth doing — poly is a wear
  item.
- The blower just sits against the cut-out on a simple shelf. No ducting needed,
  which is why the cheaper bare blowers are fine.

## Why two

One per cooler, or one per pallet position. Two lets you cool a batch while the
second is loading, which matters on a heavy harvest day — the whole point is to
pull field heat out **in the first hours**, not whenever a unit frees up.
