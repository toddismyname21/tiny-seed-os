# PART 1 — HOW THE PAYMENT WORKS

## It is NOT a reimbursement. It is a calculated rate.

This is the part that has not been explained clearly, and it changes everything.

**NRCS does not pay you back for receipts.** Every practice is broken into named
**scenarios**, and each scenario has a **published rate** — per acre, per foot, or
a flat amount per instance. **You are paid the rate.** What you actually spend is
a separate question.

### The proof, from the rate schedule itself

Several scenarios are literally named **"Foregone Income"**:

| Scenario | Rate |
|---|---|
| 332 Contour Buffer Strips — **Specialty Crops, Foregone Income** | $2,203.40/ac |
| 327 Conservation Cover — **Native Mix Plus Pollinator, Foregone Income** | $1,061.41/ac |
| 386 Field Border — **Pollinator, Foregone Income** | $1,006.03/ac |

**You cannot produce a receipt for income you did not earn.** These are
*calculated compensation* — materials, plus labour, plus the production you give
up. That is what every NRCS scenario rate is: a standard figure, paid whether or
not your spend matches it.

## 🔴 THE ONE THING TO CONFIRM — and it is not about caps

Pasa's slide says **"80% of total project cost paid up front."** The phrase is
*total project cost.*

| If it means… | Then… |
|---|---|
| **the NRCS scenario rate** *(how EQIP works)* | everything in Part 2 below holds. Where the rate exceeds your spend, you keep the difference |
| **your actual invoiced cost** | you cannot come out ahead, and this is cost-share |

**I believe it is the scenario rate** — because the practice list is NRCS's, the
rates are NRCS's, and NRCS scenarios include foregone-income components that have
no invoice. **But I have not seen Pasa state it.**

➡️ **Ask Luka this, and it is a perfectly normal question that invites no
limit:** *"How is the practice payment calculated — off the NRCS scenario rate,
or off my actual invoices?"*

## ⭐⭐ 345 — Residue & Tillage Management, Reduced — ADAPTIVE MANAGEMENT

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pays** | **$6,075.13 flat** |
| **What you buy** | **nothing** |
| **Gap** | **+$6,075** |

**What it actually is:** reducing tillage intensity and managing crop residue,
with a written plan, monitoring, and documented adjustment.

**What you do on this farm:** this *is* the tarping plan. You stop tilling to
terminate a crop and use occultation instead. You are already moving this way.

**Why the gap is real and not a trick:** the payment compensates **management
burden and risk**, not materials. Going no-till organically is genuinely harder —
you take on weed risk and you must plan. That is what the money is for.

⚠️ **The cost is your time**, not a purchase: planning, monitoring, record-keeping.

## 484 — Mulching (Synthetic Material) — ⚠️ READ THIS ONE CAREFULLY

| Scenario | Pays/ac | Your cost/ac | **Gap** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Synthetic Material** | $3,003.55 | **$3,920** ✅ | 🔴 **−$917** |
| **Synthetic Material, ROW** | **$7,461.39** | **$3,920** ✅ | ✅ **+$3,541** |

**Your tarp cost is verified** — silage tarp runs **$0.08–$0.10/sq ft**, and an
acre is 43,560 sq ft, so roughly **$3,920 an acre at $0.09.**

🔴 **At the plain per-acre rate, mulching an acre LOSES you $917.** At the Row
rate it makes $3,541.

**This single question is worth $4,458 per acre.** It is the most financially
significant unanswered item in the whole conservation plan.

> 💡 **And tarps last 3–5 years.** If the payment is annual and the tarp is
> amortised, the economics improve a lot even at the lower rate. **Ask whether
> 484 can be claimed in more than one year.**
